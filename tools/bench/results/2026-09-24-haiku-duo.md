## Limitations

- n=1 per ticket and arm. Three agent sessions on one ticket differ by more than most of the gaps in these tables, which is why every number is a median with its interquartile range beside it and why a gap smaller than that spread is not a result.
- One model (haiku) and one fixture (alan2207/bulletproof-react (apps/react-vite), a TypeScript React app). Nothing here transfers to another model or to a Python or Go repo without being run again.
- The chat is a stand-in, not the Company server: `dm-mcp.mjs` over one JSONL file, with the app’s own tool names and its descriptions word for word, but none of the real thing’s SQLite, channels, pins, attachments or engine. What an Employee reads and writes is the same; what the app does around it is not.
- The protocol is the bench’s. The lead is told to DM the colleague twice — before it writes code, and when the change is ready — and every DM wakes the other, up to five wakes a cell. A real Company decides for itself how much to talk, and an arm that talked less because it was told to would be a measurement of the instruction.
- Both arms carry the app’s How you write section and differ by Between Employees alone. The tool descriptions name the Company’s voice setting the way the app’s do, identically in both arms.
- Two Employees in one checkout, and the colleague is told not to write files. A real Company gives a reviewer a worktree of its own.
- Whether the ticket landed is a green suite, code in the diff, and both Employees having written. It is not a review of the code, and the DM columns are characters and words with an estimate in tokens beside them (characters ÷ 4), never a count from a tokenizer.
- Not measured: whether the feature asked for was actually built, and whether it was built well. The fixture’s own suite is a regression guard — it catches a change that broke the repo — and it passes for a session that wrote nothing at all, so read `lines +` beside it, not instead of it.
- Not measured: quality, safety, or the review rounds a worse diff would cost downstream. An arm that spends 20% fewer tokens and needs a second attempt has saved nothing.
- Dependencies are installed once and every workspace links to that one tree, so no cell pays for `npm install`. Each cell checks the tree is unchanged afterwards and says so if it is not.
- Every cell’s isolation was checked against its own transcript and held.
